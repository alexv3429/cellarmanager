## 2022 retained; 2023 requires a fresh review

The owner initially supplied two-year synthesis estimates using the workbook's
2021 pairs as a reference, then **withdrew the proposed 2022 replacements**
after seeing how much they differed from the workbook. Keep the existing 2022
values unchanged. The 2023 numbers from the same synthesis remain unconfirmed
leads for fresh review, not publication candidates. None of these exact years
was published by the cited organisations.

The workbook's standard/premium flags are **not** verified synonyms for
non-Grand-Cru/Grand-Cru or generic/Cru. The dates in its 2021 and 2022 columns
are age offsets converted to calendar years (`Millesimes!L9:O10` and
`Millesimes!L19:O20`). The 2023 source rows `Millesimes!L11:O11` and
`Millesimes!L21:O21` are empty. The suggested 2023 Beaujolais dates are much
earlier than the retained 2022 workbook dates, so recheck them independently
instead of carrying forward the withdrawn 2022 assumptions.

The vintage rationale is directionally supported by [Jancis Robinson's Alsace
chart](https://www.jancisrobinson.com/learn/vintages/alsace), the [Beaujolais
interprofession's 2022–2023 assessments](https://www.beaujolais.com/deguster/conseils-de-degustation/les-millesimes/),
and the Alsace interprofession's distinct [Winzenberg](https://www.vinsalsace.com/fr/grands-crus/winzenberg/)
and [Schlossberg](https://www.vinsalsace.com/fr/grands-crus/schlossberg/)
ageing descriptions. These sources do **not** publish exact 2023 windows for
the proposed categories.
The Grand Cru and Cru categories are not automatic wildcards: Alsace grape,
dryness and site, and Beaujolais appellation and cuvée structure can change
the useful window. Exclude Vendanges Tardives, SGN, Beaujolais Nouveau and
simple wines outside the reviewed group. Each wine needs verified eligibility.

For 2023, define group membership, research each category independently, and
review first-trial and preferably-drink-by years alongside the two likely-best
years. Do not interpolate from 2021 or 2022, infer the outer years
mechanically, or publish the initial two-date suggestions as completed shared
references. The current model can remain a clearly labelled fallback while
these profiles are unresolved.

The five complete two-year Chagnoleau producer-only rows (2016, 2017, 2018,
2020, and 2022) are a **different identity gap**, not missing dates: neither
cuvée nor appellation is specified.
Research cannot safely attach them to one release or producer-appellation key
while those fields are unknown. They stay quarantined pending the owner's
intended scope and canonical identity review.

## Decision requested

1. Preserve the two Castagnier not-before notes without interpolation. Use
   separately researched, exact-wine evidence for any complete replacement
   window; do not translate `Bourgogne GC` into an appellation wildcard.
2. Decide the intended identity/scope of the Chagnoleau rows. Without it, do
   not attach their existing years to an arbitrary wine.
3. Retain the workbook's 2022 pairs. Define Alsace-white and Beaujolais-red
   ageing groups and eligible wines, then reassess the unfilled 2023 rows
   independently. A broad `all-at-this-scope` group requires affirmative
   evidence, not merely a blank group field.
4. For every resulting exact or broader candidate, review **first trial /
   likely best start / likely best end / preferably drink by** together. The
   external two-date ranges above are leads, not automatically equivalent to
   the workbook's min/max or to four approved milestones.

Until those decisions are made, keep these candidates unpublished. An
unmatched wine may continue to use the clearly labelled existing model
estimate where its safety checks pass; otherwise show no window. This review
does not change the application or its shared reference database.
